A promise kept since 1859

In June 1859, Jean Joseph Demoulin and his wife Catherine Wera had this chapel built at their own expense, to fulfil a granted vow. On 21 August that year, for the Saint Roch procession, the statue of Our Lady of Sorrows was placed inside.

It is a small neoclassical chapel: a square brick plan, limestone imposts, a slate pavilion roof with flared eaves and, inside, groin vaults. Its door is the original. It is recorded in Wallonia's heritage inventory.

Neighbours still walk in freely to light a candle. The small plaques reading "Remerciements pour une grâce obtenue" (thanks for a grace received) say what it means to them.

In 1859, a couple from Haccourt kept their promise. Help us keep ours.

Sources: parish archives; AWaP, Wallonia's heritage inventory, record 62079-INV-0102-01.

Questions

Where does the money go?

Into a savings account opened for the chapel, in the name of its owner, Francine Thunissen. It is used only for the work. We will post progress and spending here.

What if you raise more, or less?

The work is done in order: the roof, the door, then the walls. Any money left over goes to the next job: the interior paintwork, then the statue.

When will the work start?

Once the money for the roof is in and the Walloon Region has answered our request for a small-heritage grant: the rules forbid starting before its decision. We will announce the date here.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

No. The chapel belongs to a family, not to a registered charity.

Who is organising this?

Francine Thunissen, the chapel's owner, with her family.
